Described by David Bruce Wilson in 1996 in his paper "Generating Random Spanning Trees More Quickly than the Cover Time."

The algorithm uses a Loop-Erased Random Walk (LERW):

  • Mark one cell as the maze
  • Pick any unvisited cell; random walk until you hit the maze
  • Erase any loops in the walk, then carve the path into the maze
  • Repeat until every cell is connected

Unlike recursive backtracking or Prim's, Wilson's samples uniformly over all possible spanning trees — every maze is equally probable. Pathfinding uses A* with Manhattan distance heuristic from the cyan start (top-left) to the gold goal (bottom-right).
