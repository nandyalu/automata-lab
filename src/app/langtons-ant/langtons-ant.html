Devised by Christopher Langton in 1986 while researching artificial life at Los Alamos National Laboratory. It became one of the canonical examples in the study of emergent computation.

The ant follows two rules on a grid of black and white cells:

  • On a white cell — turn right, flip it black, move forward
  • On a black cell — turn left, flip it white, move forward

The first ~10,000 steps appear chaotic and unpredictable. Then, without any change in the rules, the ant locks into a periodic "highway" that repeats forever. It remains an open mathematical question whether every starting configuration eventually produces a highway.
