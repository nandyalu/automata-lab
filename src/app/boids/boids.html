Created by Craig Reynolds and presented at SIGGRAPH 1987 in his paper "Flocks, Herds, and Schools: A Distributed Behavioral Model." Reynolds wanted to show that realistic collective motion needed no choreographer — only local rules.

Each boid steers by three forces:

  • Separation — avoid crowding nearby neighbors
  • Alignment — match the average heading of the neighborhood
  • Cohesion — move toward the neighborhood's center of mass

Boids was used in Batman Returns (1992) for the bat and penguin swarm sequences — one of the first uses of autonomous agents in film VFX. Reynolds received an Academy Award for Technical Achievement in 1998. The model has since influenced robotics, AI, and the study of biological swarms.
