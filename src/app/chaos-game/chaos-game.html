Popularised by mathematician Michael Barnsley in his 1988 book Fractals Everywhere as a way to generate fractals through randomness. The underlying triangle was first described by Wacław Sierpiński in 1915.

The algorithm: pick a random vertex, jump partway toward it, plot the point — repeat.

  • 3 vertices, ratio 0.5 — the Sierpiński triangle
  • Adjusting vertices or jump ratio produces entirely different fractals

Barnsley's insight was that deterministic fractal structures can be recovered from a random process — a discovery that led directly to fractal image compression, used commercially in the 1990s to store images at far higher ratios than JPEG. The fractal is self-similar at every scale: zoom into any corner and the whole triangle reappears.
