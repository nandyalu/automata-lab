First described by Peter Gray & Stephen K. Scott in 1984 (Chemical Engineering Science). The pattern possibilities were mapped by John Pearson in his landmark 1993 paper "Complex Patterns in a Simple System."

Two chemicals U and V react and diffuse across a surface:

  • U is continuously fed into the system (rate f)
  • U + 2V → 3V — V autocatalyzes its own production
  • V is killed at rate k

The tension between these rates generates strikingly different patterns — spots, stripes, spirals, and mazes — from the same two equations. This connects directly to Alan Turing's 1952 theory of morphogenesis, which proposed that chemical gradients alone can explain animal coat patterns like leopard spots and zebra stripes.
